# Same chain, same boxes, new weights

**The reviewer's question:** did SELF-SALIENCY learn to attend to the regions its reasoning
names, or did it learn to write reasoning that its existing attention already satisfies?
The trained model changes how it generates — shorter chains, somewhat larger grounded
regions — so any number computed on its own generations confounds the two.

**The design that separates them.** Take held-out reasoning chains, feed *exactly the same
chain* to both models under teacher forcing, score *exactly the same* Grounding-DINO boxes
with attention from *exactly the same* heads. The model is not allowed to change what it
says, so anything left is attention.

This page runs that in both directions — the cold start's chains through both models, and
SELF-SALIENCY's chains through both models — so the answer cannot depend on whose chains
were picked. Companion pages: [selfground-hacking.md](selfground-hacking.md) (did the
*text* move?) and [reasoning-alignment.md](reasoning-alignment.md) (does the attention land
on the region at all?).

## The answer in five lines

1. **On the reward's own metric, nothing moved.** φ at the two rewarded heads, chain and
   boxes fixed: −0.0005 [−0.0015, +0.0005]. Normalising within the visual tokens does not
   rescue it (−0.0002 [−0.0020, +0.0017]); neither does the step-level sign test (48.2%).
2. **The confound the reviewer names is real, and it is mostly text.** The trained model
   does send more attention to image tokens — 2.31× at the rewarded heads on its own
   generations. Held fixed, the chain accounts for 1.88× of that and the weights for
   1.23× \* — three quarters of the multiple, in log terms, is a fact about the sentences.
3. **But at the full layer-22 map the weights really did redistribute attention into the
   named region**, chain and boxes fixed: in-region share of visual attention
   +0.0081 [+0.0063, +0.0099] \*, enrichment 1.202 → 1.233 \* (chance 1.0), on 68.8% of
   steps. Normalised within the visual tokens, so it is not line 2 in disguise.
4. **It survives both matched nulls.** Against the same union translated over the grid:
   +0.025 [+0.018, +0.033] \*. Against the *other* observation steps' unions on the same
   picture: +0.010 [+0.007, +0.012] \*. So it is neither the region's geometry nor a
   general drift toward where named regions tend to sit.
5. **Most of it is not step-specific, though.** Two thirds of the enrichment gain is shared
   with the chain's *other* regions; the step-specific remainder wins on 55.3%
   [52.9, 57.7] of steps on the cold start's chains and on 52.4% [49.6, 55.3] — not
   distinguishable from chance — on SELF-SALIENCY's own.

The one-sentence version: **the attention did move, and in the right direction, but not at
the heads the reward reads and not on the statistic the reward optimises.**

## The setting

| | |
|---|---|
| prompts | the trainer's own 100-row holdout of `peterant330/saliency-r1-8k` (`test_size=100, seed=42`) |
| chains | two sets of 800 completions, 8 rollouts per prompt at T = 1.0, ≤1024 new tokens, generated by `overlap_probe.py` (`align-A` = cold start, `align-C` = `ours`) |
| completions used | 769 (cold start) + 754 (`ours`) — those with at least one grounded observation step |
| steps scored | 2,664 from the cold start's chains and 1,491 from `ours`', each read through **both** models: 8,310 (step, model) scores |
| boxes | the unions the reward itself saw, decoded from the probe file rather than re-grounded, so the mask is bit-identical across models |
| attention | layer 22, heads 28+31 (the rewarded heads), from the same eager re-run of layer 22 the reward uses on the teacher-forced `prompt ++ completion` forward; and, off the same capture, the mean over all 32 heads |
| intervals | prompt-clustered bootstrap over the 100 prompts; deltas paired on the prompt, starred when the interval excludes zero |

Two receipts that the cells are what they claim. The **diagonal reproduces the probe**:
re-read φ 0.0504 against stored 0.0504, r = 1.000 over 4,155 steps — the adapter is
loaded and the capture is the reward's. And the run is **deterministic**: an independent
second submission reproduced all 8,310 rows of every shared field to 0.0e+00.

## The metrics, and why four of them

For a step's map `sal` over the patch grid `I` and its grounded union `U`:

| | | chance |
|---|---|---|
| `phi` | `mean(sal over U) / max(sal over I)` — the paper's reward | unknown |
| `in-region share` | `mass(U) / mass(I)` — normalised **within** the visual tokens | `|U|/|I|` |
| `enrichment` | that share divided by `|U|/|I|` — scale-free (this is `mean_in_v2`) | 1.0 |
| `visual attention` | `mass(I)` as a fraction of the token's whole attention row | — |

`phi` divides by the map's own peak, so it can be raised by flattening the map — which is
[how 88% of the text effect gets in](selfground-hacking.md). `in-region share` and
`enrichment` divide by the map's mass on the image, so they measure **where on the image
the attention went** and cannot be bought by sending more attention to the image. That last
quantity is the confound, so it gets its own column rather than being divided out silently.

with the normalisation the reviewer asked for, which does not change it.

## 2. The confound, measured rather than assumed

`visual attention` = the share of the step's attention row that lands on image tokens.

| chains from | attention from | rewarded heads | all 32 heads |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold start | cold start | 0.0065 [0.0053, 0.0079] | 0.0618 [0.0588, 0.0649] |
| cold start | SELF-SALIENCY | 0.0079 [0.0065, 0.0095] | 0.0688 [0.0658, 0.0721] |
| | Δ | +0.0014 [+0.0012, +0.0016] \* | +0.0071 [+0.0067, +0.0075] \* |
| SELF-SALIENCY | cold start | 0.0122 [0.0096, 0.0152] | 0.0684 [0.0649, 0.0721] |
| SELF-SALIENCY | SELF-SALIENCY | 0.0150 [0.0120, 0.0184] | 0.0768 [0.0733, 0.0804] |
| | Δ | +0.0027 [+0.0023, +0.0032] \* | +0.0084 [+0.0078, +0.0089] \* |

Over the whole `<think>` span rather than the observation steps, per completion, the same
picture: 0.0040 → 0.0048 \* at the rewarded heads and 0.0356 → 0.0395 \* at all heads with
the cold start's chains held fixed. The trained model beats the cold start on this in 94.0%
of steps at the rewarded heads and 97.1% at all heads.

**The decomposition.** At the rewarded heads, comparing each model on its own generations —
the number a reader of the paper would compute — gives **2.31×** more visual attention.
Split it:

| | factor |
|---|---|
| text (cold-start weights, its chains vs the cold start's) | **1.88×** |
| weights (chain held fixed) | **1.23×** \* |
| product | **2.31×** — the observed multiple, exactly |

Taking the other path through the 2×2 gives 1.22× for the weights, so the split does not
depend on the order. The large increase in overall visual attention is **three quarters a
property of the sentences** (in log terms) — longer observation steps, differently
distributed — and a quarter a real change in the weights. Both are true; only the second is
a claim about attention. (The string "2.53" does not appear in the submitted PDF, so the
multiple above is what this corpus gives rather than a reproduction of that number; the
split is the part that matters and it does not depend on which multiple you start from.)

## 3. What did move: the full layer-22 map

Same chains, same boxes, same forward — only the head set is wider.

| chains from | attention from | φ | in-region share | enrichment | enr − roll null | enr − sibling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cold start | cold start | 0.1576 | 0.5898 [0.5554, 0.6242] | 1.202 [1.154, 1.252] | 0.200 [0.153, 0.248] | 0.136 [0.110, 0.163] |
| cold start | SELF-SALIENCY | 0.1540 | 0.5979 [0.5646, 0.6325] | 1.233 [1.180, 1.288] | 0.225 [0.174, 0.279] | 0.146 [0.117, 0.175] |
| | Δ | −0.0036 \* | **+0.0081 [+0.0063, +0.0099] \*** | **+0.031 [+0.023, +0.040] \*** | **+0.025 [+0.018, +0.033] \*** | **+0.010 [+0.007, +0.012] \*** |
| SELF-SALIENCY | cold start | 0.1571 | 0.6269 [0.5907, 0.6600] | 1.180 [1.137, 1.226] | 0.168 [0.127, 0.212] | 0.116 [0.090, 0.145] |
| SELF-SALIENCY | SELF-SALIENCY | 0.1516 | 0.6346 [0.5984, 0.6674] | 1.204 [1.156, 1.255] | 0.188 [0.144, 0.237] | 0.122 [0.094, 0.152] |
| | Δ | −0.0055 \* | **+0.0077 [+0.0057, +0.0097] \*** | **+0.025 [+0.019, +0.031] \*** | **+0.020 [+0.015, +0.026] \*** | **+0.006 [+0.004, +0.008] \*** |

With the sentence and the boxes nailed down, the trained weights put more of the layer's
visual attention inside the region the sentence names. In above-chance terms the enrichment
excess goes 0.202 → 0.233, a **15% increase in localisation**, on 68.8% of steps.

**The two nulls this has to survive.** The named regions of this corpus are large (the
union covers 53% of the grid on average) and sit nearer the centre than the attention does,
so "more mass inside the union" could be geometry rather than grounding:

- **roll null** — the step's *own* union translated over the grid, area and shape fixed,
  up to 16 in-frame offsets (13.6 on average), the same offsets for both models (seeded
  from the step's identity, so the comparison stays paired). Gain survives: +0.025 \*,
  65.1% of steps.
- **sibling null** — the union of a *different* observation step of the same completion:
  same picture, same chain, same weights, only the region changes. Gain survives in the
  mean: +0.010 \*, on the 88.1% of steps that have a distinct sibling.

Note what the sibling null costs: of the +0.031 raw enrichment gain, only +0.010 is
step-specific. And its sign test is weak — 55.3% [52.9, 57.7] of steps on the cold start's
chains, 52.4% [49.6, 55.3] (not significant) on SELF-SALIENCY's own. **The honest claim is
"attention moved toward the kinds of regions the chain names", not "attention tracks each
step".** That matches the held-out alignment eval, where step-specificity at the rewarded
heads is a coin flip for this arm.

φ goes the *other* way at all heads (−0.0036 \*) because the full-head map gets peakier
(map mean/max 0.1352 → 0.1298 \*), and φ divides by the peak. One more reason not to read φ
as a localisation measure.

**Is the in-region gain significant?** Tested on the 100 prompt means rather than the 4,155
steps — the 8 rollouts of a prompt see one picture, so they are not 8 draws:

| | cold start's chains | SELF-SALIENCY's chains |
|---|---|---|
| mean paired difference | +0.0082 | +0.0087 |
| paired *t* | t(99) = 8.61, p = 1.2e-13 | t(98) = 7.84, p = 5.6e-12 |
| Wilcoxon signed-rank | p = 1.3e-11 | p = 2.2e-10 |
| sign test over prompts | 82/100 up, p = 6.2e-11 | 77/99 up, p = 2.5e-08 |
| cluster sign-flip permutation | p < 5e-06 (200k draws) | p < 5e-06 |
| Cohen's *d* over prompts | 0.86 | 0.79 |

Counting each prompt once: +0.0084, t(98) = 8.47, p = 2.5e-13. The tiny p-values come from
**consistency, not size** — 0.0084 on a base of 0.612 is a 1.4% relative move, which is the
15% figure only because chance eats most of the baseline.

Figure 5's corner effect is real and is a weight effect — the top-left sink drops 17% with

and the flattening live; the localisation gain lives everywhere else.

## 5. One number per model

A 2×2 is the right design but the wrong table for a paper. To collapse it without
favouring either side, average each model over the two chain sources with **equal weight**,
per prompt: both models then read the same corpus — half the cold start's prose, half
SELF-SALIENCY's — so nothing in the number depends on whose sentences they are. (Do *not*
pool by concatenating all 4,155 steps: 64% of them come from the cold start's chains,
because it writes more observation steps per completion, so that mix silently favours one
side.) 99 prompts; one holdout prompt has no usable step in both chain sets.

**Rewarded heads, layer 22 h28+31**

| model | φ | in-region share | overall visual attention |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold start | 0.0533 | 0.4620 | 0.0096 |
| SELF-SALIENCY | 0.0529 | 0.4622 | **0.0119** |
| Δ | −0.0004 [−0.0014, +0.0006] | +0.0002 [−0.0014, +0.0019] | **+0.0022 [+0.0019, +0.0025]** |
| | p = 0.45 | p = 0.8 | p = 1.4e-25, 99/99 prompts |

**All 32 heads of layer 22**

| model | φ | in-region share | overall visual attention |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold start | 0.1574 | 0.6117 | 0.0647 |
| SELF-SALIENCY | 0.1528 | **0.6201** | **0.0726** |
| Δ | −0.0046 \* | **+0.0084 [+0.0064, +0.0103]** | **+0.0078 [+0.0074, +0.0082]** |
| | | p = 2.5e-13, 78/99 | p = 1.5e-60, 99/99 |

Read the last two columns together and the whole page is in one place. At the rewarded
heads the trained model sends **1.23× more** attention to the image — the most consistent
effect anywhere here, 99 prompts out of 99 — and **none of it** is aimed: the in-region
share is 0.4620 against 0.4622. At the full layer the volume rises 1.12× *and* the region
gets more than its share on top of that. Volume is the column that moves most and says
least.

A stronger version of this table, if it is worth another ~8-minute job: chains written by
**neither** model. `no_sal` — the same GRPO run with `R_sal` removed — already has 800
completions on these same 100 prompts in `align-A`, so `--text-arm no_sal --map-arm
base_coldstart --map-arm ours` gives one number per model off a third-party corpus, with no
averaging argument needed at all.

## What this means for the paper

The experiment does not damage Table 1 or Table 4 — those are accuracy, and nothing here
touches them. It changes what can be said about the *mechanism*, and it gives a defensible
version of the mechanism claim that the paper currently does not make:

- **Do not** claim that SELF-SALIENCY raises φ by attending better. The fixed-chain φ is
  flat to ±0.001; the gain the paper reports is carried by the sentences.
- **Do not** quote an overall "visual attention increased N×" without splitting it: on this
  corpus ~80% of that multiple is the text.
- **Do** claim, with this table behind it, that with the reasoning chain and the grounding
  boxes held fixed, SELF-SALIENCY's layer-22 attention is **15% more enriched on the named
  regions**, that this survives a translation null and a sibling-region null, and that it
  is measured after normalising within the visual tokens so it is redistribution and not
  volume.
- **Do** say plainly that the effect is not visible at the two heads the reward is computed
  on. That is a strange fact and a reviewer will find it; stating it first is much cheaper
  than defending it later. The natural reading — 2 of the layer's 32 heads got a direct
  gradient and the rest of the layer moved with them — is also a testable claim, and the
  obvious follow-up is the same table at a few other layers.

## Reproducing

```fish
# ~8 min on 8 GPUs, twice (the second adds the nulls; the first is the determinism check)
bash launch_selfground_audit_job.sh --name sg-fixedchain --stage crosspass \
    --out-dir outputs/selfground/fixedchain_nulls --gpus 8 --duration 1 -- \
    --probe outputs/overlap_probe/align-A --probe outputs/overlap_probe/align-C \
    --keep-arm base_coldstart --keep-arm ours --base base_coldstart --all-heads --nulls
python selfground_audit.py --stage report --out-dir outputs/selfground/fixedchain_nulls \
    --base base_coldstart
```

Raw tables: `outputs/selfground/fixedchain_nulls/report.md` §7. Per-step rows (one per
step per model, with every column above) are in that directory's `crosspass.json`. Adding
an arm is one more `--probe`/`--keep-arm`; `center_rect`, `question_boxes`, `no_sal` and
`mean_in_v2` all have generations on the same 100 prompts.
