Where in the picture does attention go?

The image border draws about 2.6× its fair share of attention in every one of twelve image types — so it is not the background. Turning the picture upside down does not move it — so it is not the content. Permuting which patch embedding sits in which grid slot does move it, and the peak follows the embedding — so it is not the language model’s position either. The mark is stamped into the patch vector by the vision tower.

1  The border wins in all twelve types

Enrichment = the border’s share of the attention spent inside the picture, divided by the border’s share of the patches. 1.00 = its fair share. Bars are the mean over pictures; the whisker is a 95% bootstrap interval over pictures. Cold start, 150 pictures per type, at 16 heads chosen on held-out images.

The ordering is the argument. The highest is the synthetic pop-out field, a uniform array of shapes where “background” is not a place. The lowest are the science diagrams and math figures — the types with the most white space. That is backwards for an account in which attention seeks the background.

2  It is a cliff, not a slope

Enrichment by distance from the nearest edge, pooled over types. Attention does not fade inward — it drops eightfold at the first step off the border and is flat all the way to the centre.

3  And it is really the top-left corner

The border is not evenly favoured. Log scale, because the single top-left patch is off any linear axis the rest would share. The dashed line is the fair share.

The bottom row is below its fair share, and one patch — the top-left, which is the first image token in raster order and sits right after the image delimiter — takes more than seventy times its share. A symmetric 2-D border effect has no reason to do that.

4  Every location, every type

Diverging around the fair share: ■ blue = above, ■ red = below, gray = exactly 1.00. Colour is on a log scale so 2× and ½× sit equally far from the middle. The two single-patch columns are in figure 3, on their own scale.

5  Which heads, and which tokens were asking

Vanilla Qwen3-VL-8B. Nothing here is head-selected: the two series are every head in the model and the two heads the reward trains on (layer 22, heads 28 and 31). “Generated” is one teacher-forced pass over prompt + answer — the same construction the training reward uses.

Two things this says. Prefill overstates the reward’s view by about a third — any saliency number measured on prompt tokens alone is an upper bound, not an estimate. And the two trained heads are not typical heads: they lean on the border harder than the model’s average head on every token set, so mean_in measures where two unusually edge-leaning heads look.

6  The causal arms

Same picture, one thing changed, paired against its own baseline. Change in border enrichment; the whisker is a 95% bootstrap interval over pictures. Cold start, 480 pictures.

7  Does the peak follow the content, or the grid slot?

Share of pictures whose peak patch shows the baseline’s peak content versus whose peak sits at the same grid position. For a positional effect the second is high and the first is at chance.

Every pixel-space transform keeps the slot and abandons the content. Then the permutation — which touches no pixel, only which slot holds which patch vector — does the exact opposite. That pair is the whole mechanism argument.

8  The hypotheses, scored

H1–H4 were written down before the run. H5 was not — it is what H1 and H3 become once the permutation arm is read, so this run generated it and cannot also confirm it. Its predictions are written in falsifiable form so another model can fail them.

Caveats

  • One model family. Qwen3-VL-8B, base and cold start. The cross-model stage is built and unrun.
  • Images capped at 512px on the long side, so grids are at most 16 patches across and the ring is a thick band. Halving the resolution costs 0.66 of border enrichment — resolution is not neutral, and nothing above 512 was tested.
  • The arms ran on the cold start only. Figure 5’s base-vs-cold-start agreement is the reason to expect they carry over, not evidence that they do.
  • It is a peak, not a sink. The strongest image column takes 33–51× uniform but its across-query variation never falls below 1.2, against a pre-registered 0.5. The word “sink” does not fit what is on the border.
  • H5 is post hoc. Figures 1–4 and 6–7 are not.
